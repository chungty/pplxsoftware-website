Comparison

How TeamOps compares to the alternatives.

A page for HR ops managers and first ops hires at Jira-using teams. We are not the right pick for everyone. Here is an honest map of what your other options are, and when each one is the right call.

The four alternatives most teams consider.

For each one, what it does well — and what we hear from teams that outgrew it.

Alternative 1

A shared spreadsheet plus Slack DMs.

This is what most teams are actually doing today. It is free. It is flexible. Anyone on the team can edit it. For a team of five, it works.

It stops working when balances start to drift. Three copies of "PTO Tracker FINAL v2" appear. Someone burns a PTO day on a national holiday. Approval requests get lost in a Slack scroll. Reconciling the sheet at month-end becomes a 3-hour job. There is no audit trail when a manager asks who approved what.

Alternative 2

Tempo Timesheets and similar time-tracking apps.

Tempo is excellent at what it does — tracking hours worked against projects, billing clients, capacity planning for engineering teams. If you bill clients hourly, you probably already use it.

It solves a different problem than TeamOps. Tempo tracks time spent. TeamOps tracks time off. The two are complementary, not competing — many teams run both. If you need to know who logged 8 hours to which project, that is Tempo. If you need to know who is out next week and how much PTO they have left, that is TeamOps.

Alternative 3

BambooHR, Lattice, Personio, and other full HR tools.

These are real HRIS platforms. They handle performance reviews, benefits administration, payroll, compensation bands, document storage, and compliance reporting. If you have 200 people and a People team, you need one of these.

They run $10 to $22 per user per month. The evaluation cycle is weeks of demos, security review, and procurement. And they live outside the tool your team already opens every day. Use a real HRIS when you need one. Use TeamOps until you do.

Alternative 4

Native Jira with custom fields and workflows.

Jira is configurable. A capable Jira admin can build a request type for time off, route it through an approval workflow, and store the result on an issue. We have seen teams ship a working version in a week.

What they cannot build is a leave balance, a holiday-aware business-day count, or a team availability summary. These are not configuration gaps — they are architectural limits. The capability table below shows where the line is.

Where native Jira hits an architectural limit.

These are capabilities that no amount of automation rules, custom fields, or Confluence calendars can produce. They are why TeamOps exists as a separate app.

When TeamOps is the right call — and when it is not.

We would rather you pick the right tool than the wrong one.

Pick TeamOps when

  • Your team is roughly 5 to 100 people.
  • Your team already uses Jira every day, and you would rather not add a separate HR app to maintain.
  • You need PTO tracking, onboarding, and offboarding — not performance reviews, payroll, or benefits.

Pick something else when

  • You are 200 people or more, and you need performance reviews, payroll, and benefits in one system. Pick a real HRIS — BambooHR, Lattice, Personio, or similar.
  • You need an applicant-tracking system (ATS) or recruiting workflows. TeamOps starts the day someone signs the offer; pre-hire isn't covered.
  • You need payroll integration today. TeamOps doesn't process payroll or sync with payroll providers.
  • You need shift scheduling for hourly workers. TeamOps is built for salaried employees on PTO accrual; hourly shift management is a different problem.
  • You run multi-entity payroll across countries. TeamOps tracks one employee per Jira user; multi-entity legal structures need a real HRIS.
  • Your team does not use Jira. TeamOps lives inside Jira; if your team is not there already, this is not the right tool yet.
